# What a thief can do with your phone while it is locked

[Español](../es/phone-locked.md)

If your phone is stolen with the screen locked and the thief does not know your passcode, **they cannot get into your apps, your photos or your bank**. But there are things they can do without unlocking it. A setting prevents almost all of them.

## What they can do

### Read what shows on the lock screen

If notifications show their content while the phone is locked, messages and the verification codes that arrive by text message are visible. Those codes can be used to reset accounts.

- **How to prevent it:** show the content only when the phone is unlocked.
  - iPhone: Settings > Notifications > Show Previews > "When Unlocked".
  - Android: Settings > Notifications > Notifications on lock screen > hide sensitive content.

### Take out the SIM and use it in another phone

With your SIM in another phone, they receive your text messages and calls. It is the most direct way to get the codes for your accounts.

- **How to prevent it:** set a SIM PIN. It is asked every time the phone restarts or the card moves to another device. It also works for eSIM.
- If your phone is stolen, ask your carrier to block the SIM as soon as you can.

### Cut its connection

If Control Center or quick settings open while the phone is locked, the thief can turn on Airplane Mode so you stop seeing where it is.

- **iPhone:** Settings > Face ID & Passcode > Allow Access When Locked > turn off Control Center.
- **Android:** turn on Offline Device Lock (Settings > Google > All services > Theft protection). It locks the screen if the phone goes offline while unlocked.
- Even switched off, a recent phone can still be located for a while: an iPhone 11 or later, for up to 24 hours; some Android phones such as the Pixel 8, for several hours. The Find My or Find Hub network must be on.

### Trick you later

This is very common. Days or months after the theft you get a text or an email that looks like Apple or Google: "we have located your phone, sign in here". The page is fake and asks for your password, to remove the lock that stops the phone from being resold.

- **Never** type your password or a verification code after following a link in a message. Go there yourself by typing the address: icloud.com/find or android.com/find.

### See or use a few things without unlocking

- **Your Medical ID**, if you show it when locked. It is meant for emergencies: decide what you put in it.
- **Transit cards in Express Mode** (iPhone): they work without unlocking.
- **The voice assistant**, if you allow it when locked.

### Sell it for parts

Activation Lock (iPhone) and device protection (Android) stop anyone from setting the phone up again without your account. That is why many stolen phones end up taken apart. Do not remove it from your account after the theft.

## What they cannot do

- Open your apps, your email, your photos or your bank.
- Pay with Apple Pay or Google Wallet (except transit in Express Mode).
- Set the phone up as new and use it.

All of this changes if the thief **knows your passcode**. See [Can they unlock my phone?](phone-unlock.md).
