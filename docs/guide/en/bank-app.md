# Is it safe to have your bank's app on your phone?

[Español](../es/bank-app.md)

Short answer: **yes, as long as the thief does not know your phone's passcode**. With the phone locked, the banking app is safe. The risk appears when someone has the phone **and** your passcode.

> This is not banking advice. Every bank has its own limits and ways to block access: ask yours.

## Why the app is reasonably protected

In the European Union, banks must ask for at least **two different proofs** to sign in or to pay. They must be of two of these kinds:

- something you know (a PIN or a password);
- something you have (your phone or your card);
- something you are (your fingerprint or face).

That is why the app asks for its own PIN or your fingerprint on top of being installed on your phone. And that is why text-message codes have a weak point: what they prove is that you have the SIM, and the SIM can be taken out of the phone.

## When the risk is real: if they know your passcode

With the phone and the passcode, a thief can:

- **Pay with Apple Pay or Google Wallet.** Both accept the phone's passcode instead of your fingerprint or face. On iPhone this stays true even with Stolen Device Protection on.
- **See your saved passwords**, unless you have Stolen Device Protection (iPhone) or Identity Check (Android), which require your fingerprint or face away from your usual places. In the thefts documented in 2023, that is how they got into banking apps.
- **Receive recovery codes.** If your bank lets you recover access with a text message or an email, they arrive on that same phone.

## What to do

**What protects the phone** (hardenme's checklist checks these):

1. Stolen Device Protection (iPhone) or Identity Check (Android).
2. A passcode of 6 digits or more, typed in public as little as possible.
3. A SIM PIN.
4. Notifications without content while the phone is locked.

**What protects the banking app:**

5. **Lock the app with your face or fingerprint.** On iPhone (iOS 18 or later), touch and hold the icon and choose "Require Face ID". Away from home and with Stolen Device Protection, not even the passcode opens it. On Android 15 or later you can put it in the "Private space", with a different lock from the phone's.
6. **Do not keep bank passwords in notes or photos.**

**Our advice, which does not come from an official source but is common sense:**

7. Make the banking app's PIN **different** from your phone's passcode.
8. Set low limits for transfers and payments, if your bank allows it.
9. Turn on alerts for every transaction.
10. Carry a second card separately, and keep your bank's phone number **outside the phone**.

## If your phone is stolen

The first hour is the one that counts. According to INCIBE, Spain's cybersecurity institute:

1. Mark it as lost or lock it remotely. That suspends the cards in Apple Pay and Google Wallet.
2. Call your carrier to block the SIM, so codes stop arriving on the stolen phone.
3. Call your bank to block cards and access.
4. Change your email and online banking passwords, and close open sessions.
5. Report it to the police, with the IMEI. Write it down now: dial `*#06#`.

The full plan is in [My device was stolen: what to do now](theft-after.md).
